The Gap

A property's experience and its representation tend to drift apart.

Most independent properties are photographed once, early on, and represented online for years after that. The rooms get repainted. The restaurant changes hands. A wedding lawn becomes the main draw. The imagery stays the same.

Guests arrive having formed an idea of the property from a handful of images. When that idea doesn't match what they find, the gap becomes the property's problem to manage, at check-in, in reviews, in every message that starts with "it looks different in person."

EXIF exists to close that gap before it opens: reading a property honestly, then producing the images that represent what it actually offers.

Approach

Decide first. Produce second.

Read the property

Who stays here, why they book, what the property is competing against, and where the current imagery misrepresents any of that.

Decide the perception

Before a camera comes out, EXIF defines what the property should be understood as, and which spaces, moments and details carry that meaning.

Produce deliberately

Photography and film are directed toward that decision, not toward a generic list of hotel shots.

About

Three years inside hotel operations, now behind the camera.

Katia built EXIF after three years inside hotel operations and sales, working across resorts in Mexico and the Caribbean. That time was spent close to guests, sales targets, wedding production and the teams responsible for the day-to-day relationship between a property and the people who visit it.

That background is what shapes the way EXIF reads a property: from how it's actually run and sold, not only from how it looks through a lens. Photography is the medium. Understanding the business behind it is what directs where the camera points.
