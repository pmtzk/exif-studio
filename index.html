A place, made unmistakable.

Before a guest experiences a place, they experience its representation.

EXIF works with independent hospitality properties to define what should become recognizable before arrival—and produces the photography and film through which it is seen.

A guest is not admiring the property. They are comparing it.

By the time someone reaches the booking page, the place is one of six tabs. The question is no longer whether it is beautiful. Every option is beautiful.

What makes this the one worth choosing.

A read.

Every property is already saying something, and most of it was never decided. Here is one photograph, read the way a booking decision reads it.

  1. Shade is the product.

    The canopy is not background. Woven, fringed, made by hand: this is the texture the rate is actually paying for, and it should lead rather than sit above the frame.

  2. The boat reads as access.

    Moored where the guest can see it, it becomes something included. Cropped out, the property loses a reason to be chosen over the one down the beach.

  3. A place to stand inside the image.

    The daybed in the foreground lets the viewer occupy the scene. Without it the property is observed rather than entered.

  4. What is missing.

    The only people are distant. Nothing here shows service happening, so warmth and staffing stay theoretical. This is the frame the property still needs.

Read. Define. Produce.
